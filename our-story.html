OUR STORY

The game I wanted to play

How a game idea during a commute became a world of business.

By J.R., creator of Capitalist Empire

Capitalist Empire began on August 9, 2026, during a commute to work. I was bored, exploring some AI tools, and wondering how much I could make with them. I started describing a game I wanted to play. The first result already had graphics, menus and an interface I could read. Seeing that appear from a short description was exciting. It gave me something I could keep building.

The idea came from many hours with the Capitalism series, especially Capitalism Plus. I loved starting a business from very little and finding ways to grow it. Price wars, research, the stock market, investing in another company and watching it succeed: those were the things that kept me playing. I still appreciate those games and the people who keep developing them. They helped me understand what I enjoy in an economic game.

I also had a growing wish list. I wanted a world I enjoyed looking at, room to develop my own ideas, and the freedom to play wherever I happened to be. On my phone during a commute, at a computer, on a laptop or a tablet: I wanted the game to fit into my life. Making my own gave me somewhere to explore that possibility.

Building systems that make sense

I enjoy making things, especially systems. I like planning how the pieces fit together and working out why something should behave the way it does. I think of myself as an architect in that sense. The pleasure comes from seeing separate ideas become something coherent.

There had been earlier attempts at making games. During the pandemic, a friend and I worked on an idea for a hackathon. He handled the code and I worked on the art. We did not finish in time to submit it, but I liked what we had begun. Capitalist Empire became another chance to follow that interest, with AI tools making it possible to try ideas I could describe but could not easily execute on my own.

It has been frustrating at times. I have run into limits with the tools, and I have spent time chasing one problem while missing several others around it. Finding people willing to playtest an early version has been difficult, too. I sometimes enjoy building the game more than playing it, which makes another person's experience especially valuable.

One of the first moments that felt right was seeing the core economy work and a business make money. That was a real milestone for me. Then I looked at the screen and felt that the experience still needed a more appealing world around it.

Making progress visible

The graphics overhaul brought me back to the original idea. Over the past few weeks, I have started looking at the game and thinking: this is closer to what I wanted. The economy matters to me, and I want the world on screen to give that work a visible reward.

The cities are part of that. Baku is one city among several. It first appeared as a suggestion from the AI tools, and I decided to keep it. As the visual work developed, I wanted to give it a more recognizable identity. That same ambition applies to the other cities: each should feel like a place with its own character.

Starting in the early twentieth century gives that world a long period of change to explore. My longer-term ambition is for businesses to be affected by consequential events, for cities and landscapes to evolve, and for later periods to bring different economic challenges. Inflation, purchasing power and changing conditions should matter. Those are goals I want to develop carefully as the game grows.

I am still making the game I wanted to play. The next big update is another step toward that picture, and there is plenty left to learn and improve.

What I most want from someone's first session is for them to have a good time. A striking first impression can pass quickly. I would rather make something people enjoy and remember a year, two years or three years later. That is the experience I hope Capitalist Empire can become.
