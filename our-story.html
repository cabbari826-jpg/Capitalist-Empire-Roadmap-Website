OUR STORY

The game I wanted to play

How a game idea during a commute became a world of business.

By J.R., creator of Capitalist Empire

On August 9, 2026, I was bored on my commute, playing with AI tools and wondering how far they could go. So I started describing a game I wanted to play. What came back had graphics, menus and an interface I could actually read. That thrilled me. It gave me something to keep building.

I knew what I was after because of the hours I had spent with the Capitalism series, Capitalism Plus above all. I loved starting with very little and finding a way to grow: a price war, a bet on research, a turn on the stock market, a stake in another company that came good. Those games taught me what I enjoy in an economic game, and I still have a lot of respect for them and for the people who keep developing them.

I also had a wish list. I wanted a world worth looking at. I wanted room for my own ideas. And I wanted to play wherever I happened to be: on my phone during a commute, at a computer, on a laptop or a tablet. Making my own game was the way to find out if that could work.

Building systems that make sense

What I really enjoy is making systems: working out how the pieces fit, and why each one should behave the way it does. I think of myself as an architect in that sense. The pleasure comes on the day separate ideas start to hold together.

I had tried before. During the pandemic, a friend and I worked on a game for a hackathon. He wrote the code and I did the art. We missed the deadline, but I liked what we had begun. AI tools gave me another run at it, because now I could try ideas I could describe but could not easily build on my own.

It has not always been fun. I have run into the limits of the tools. I have chased one problem while missing several others right beside it. Finding people to playtest an early version is hard, and because I sometimes enjoy building the game more than playing it, someone else's experience is worth a great deal to me.

One moment stands out: the core economy worked, and a business made money. That was a real milestone. Then I looked at the screen and saw how much the game still needed a world around it.

Making progress visible

The graphics overhaul took me back to where this began. Over the past few weeks I have looked at the game and thought: this is getting close to what I wanted. The economy matters to me, and the world on screen should be the reward for that work.

The cities carry a lot of that. Baku first appeared as a suggestion from the AI tools, and I decided to keep it. As the art grew, I wanted it to look like somewhere you would recognize. The same goes for every other city: each should feel like a place with its own character.

Starting in the early twentieth century gives that world a long way to travel. In time I want consequential events to hit businesses, cities and landscapes to change, and each new period to bring its own economic problems. Inflation and purchasing power should matter. I want to build all of that carefully as the game grows.

I am still making the game I wanted to play, and there is plenty left to learn.

What I want most from someone's first session is simple: that they have a good time. A striking first impression fades quickly. I would rather make something people still enjoy and remember a year, two years or three years later. That is what I hope Capitalist Empire becomes.
